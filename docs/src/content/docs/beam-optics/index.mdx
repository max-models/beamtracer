`BeamOptics` solves steady ion beams: extraction of positive or negative ions from a plasma,
transport through electrodes and solenoids, and the space charge of several species. Nothing
depends on time: the beam is the fixed point of a field solve, a trace of the trajectories
and a deposit of their space charge, on a mesh in planar (per unit depth) or cylindrically
symmetric $(x, r)$ geometry, or in 3D.

The other simulations build on it: the [charge changes](/beamtracer/charge-changes/) of a fast
beam in a neutralizer, the [beamline](/beamtracer/beamline/) that carries the beam on to a
port or a calorimeter, and the [design searches](/beamtracer/optimisation/) that tune its
electrodes.

## How a solve works

<MethodCard name="beamtracer.model.BeamOptics" headingLevel={3} />

Each step has a page with the equations it implements, declared on the code and checked by
the tests (under "Equations" in the sidebar):

1. **[Mesh](/beamtracer/numerics/solver-mesh/).** Nodes of spacing $h$ (uniform, graded along
   the beam, or with refined boxes); the electrodes are simgeometry shapes, and the distance
   from a node to an electrode surface is bracketed in 255ths of a cell.
2. **[Poisson's equation](/beamtracer/numerics/solver-poisson/)** with the Boltzmann plasma
   species, by finite differences (Shortley-Weller next to the electrodes, the axis by
   L'Hôpital) and Newton's method, second order in $h$.
3. **[Fields](/beamtracer/numerics/solver-fields/):** the electric field on the edges between
   the nodes, at the electrode surfaces taken over the bracketed distance; the magnetic field
   of solenoids or a measured profile, expanded off the axis.
4. **[Trajectories](/beamtracer/numerics/solver-tracer/)** seeded from an emission line with a
   quasi-random Sobol beam (the same on every iteration), traced by RK4, their charge
   $I \Delta t$ deposited at the midpoint of every step.
5. **[The Vlasov iteration](/beamtracer/numerics/solver-vlasov/)** ties them together,
   relaxing the charge density until the potential stops changing:

<MethodCard name="beamtracer.solver.vlasov.vlasov_iterate" headingLevel={3} />

6. **[Beam moments](/beamtracer/numerics/solver-diagnostics/)** at diagnostic planes: rms
   emittance, Twiss parameters and the mismatch, with the trajectories of a cylindrical beam
   as rings.

In 3D the same steps run on the [3D mesh](/beamtracer/numerics/solver-mesh3d/), its
[fields](/beamtracer/numerics/solver-fields3d/) and [trajectories](/beamtracer/numerics/solver-tracer3d/);
the [beam's own magnetic field](/beamtracer/numerics/solver-self-field/) is an option in
every geometry.

## The configuration

- **Mesh and field**: a uniform node mesh with Shortley--Weller cut cells at the electrodes
  (simgeometry objects with Dirichlet potentials, `ObjectParams`), Neumann or Dirichlet faces
  (`FieldBCs`), and the electric field on the edges between nodes, interpolated bilinearly.
- **Plasma**: `plasma=ExtractionPlasma(electron_temperature, plasma_potential, initial_plasma_x)`
  adds the electrons `-rho_e exp((phi - U_p) / T_e)` for positive-ion extraction, solved with
  Newton's method. For negative ions,
  `plasma=NegativeExtractionPlasma(ion_temperature, plasma_potential, initial_plasma_x)` adds
  the positive ions `rho_p exp(-(phi - U_p) / T_p)` instead, and the negative ions and the
  electrons extracted with them are beams (`charge=-1`), traced with their own space charge.
  `rho_e` and `rho_p` default to the beams' charge density at their start, `sum J / v`: the
  plasma is neutral where the beams leave it. The current density of each beam, and so the
  ratio of co-extracted electrons to negative ions, is an input of the model, not a result.
- **Beams**: `BeamSpecies` emits trajectories from a line (current density, energy, parallel
  and transverse temperatures, quasi-random Sobol velocities); `ParticleBeam` takes
  trajectories from another simulation.
- **Magnetic field**: `solenoids` or a measured `axial_field`, expanded off the axis to
  `field_order`.
- **Field maps**: `field_map={"file": "magnets.nc"}` adds an imposed E and/or B from a
  netCDF field map (`takeout.FieldMap`) to the solved field and the solenoids, in planar
  or cylindrical geometry. The layout (`b_field_<i>` in T, `e_field_<i>` in V/m on
  `x, y` in m, `Conventions = "max-models-field-map-1.0"`) is takeout's, which picnic
  reads and writes too, so a map from either code, or one sampled from an NGSolve or FEniCSx solution,
  serves both; `FieldRegion` takes a map as its `electric` or `magnetic` field too.
- **Charge changes**: `charge_changing=ChargeChangingParams(cross_sections, densities)` lets
  a fast beam change charge state on a gas or a plasma, as in a neutralizer; see
  [charge changes](/beamtracer/charge-changes/) and the example
  [Stripping in the accelerator](/beamtracer/examples/accelerator-stripping/).
- **Space charge**: deposited at the midpoint of every step with an area-weighted linear
  shape, and reduced by `compensation` regions.
- **The beam's own magnetic field**: `self_magnetic_field=True` deposits the beam's current
  as well and adds its field, from Ampère's law of magnetostatics (`curl B = mu_0 J`, all
  that Maxwell's equations leave for a steady beam): `B_theta = mu_0 I(x, r) / (2 pi r)` in
  cylindrical geometry, `dB_z/dy = mu_0 J_x` integrated across a planar beam, and the vector
  potential (`-laplace A = mu_0 J`, `B = curl A`) in 3D. For a beam along `x` it scales the
  space-charge force by about `1 - f - beta^2` (`f` the compensated share). The change of
  the envelope is `beta^2` times that of the uncompensated space charge, whatever `f` is:
  for ions below a few MeV per nucleon it is far below the other errors, so it is off by
  default. In 2D it runs on the NumPy tracer.
- **Diagnostics**: `diagnostic_planes` give the current, rms emittance and Twiss parameters of
  each species (`model.beam_moments(x, "H+")`).

```python
from beamtracer import (
    BeamOptics,
    BeamOpticsConfig,
    BeamSpecies,
    ExtractionPlasma,
    GridParams,
)

config = BeamOpticsConfig(
    grid=GridParams(
        num_gridpoints=(241, 141), upper_bound=(12e-3, 7e-3), coordinates="cylindrical"
    ),
    objects={...},  # electrodes
    beams=[
        BeamSpecies(
            "He+",
            600.0,
            (0.0, 0.0),
            (0.0, 1.5e-3),
            mass=4.0,
            energy=5.0,
            transverse_temperature=0.5,
            num_particles=15000,
        )
    ],
    plasma=ExtractionPlasma(
        electron_temperature=5.0, plasma_potential=5.0, initial_plasma_x=0.55e-3
    ),
    diagnostic_planes=[11.9e-3],
)
model = BeamOptics(config).run()
print(model.beam_moments(11.9e-3))
```

Negative-ion extraction is the exact mirror of positive-ion extraction: negating every
potential and every beam charge gives the negated potential and the same trajectories (a test
checks this to round-off).

A configuration is written to and read from its configuration-file form with
`config.to_dict()` and `BeamOpticsConfig.from_dict(...)`. Every physical setting, in
Python or in the file, takes an astropy Quantity (`2 * u.mm`), a quantity string (`"2 mm"`)
or a bare number in its documented unit (SI, energies and temperatures in eV, masses in u),
and is stored in that unit; `to_dict` writes the quantity strings (`"0.002 m"`).

## A graded beam axis

The beam's optics are set where the potential changes fast (the meniscus, the gaps between
the grids) and hardly change along a drift. `GridParams(x_spacing=...)` grades the beam axis:
`(x, dx)` pairs give the node spacing wanted at `x`, linear between them and constant beyond,
and the nodes along `x` follow it with cells that change in size smoothly
(`patchwork.graded_nodes`); the spacing across the beam is that of `num_gridpoints` along `y`.
`x_through` puts nodes at given positions, the faces and tips of the electrodes, so that their
surfaces fall on the mesh. The field solve (patchwork's `NodalPoisson` on a graded axis), the
staggered field, the space charge and both tracers use the physical cell sizes; refinement
boxes split the graded cells evenly.

```python
grid = GridParams(
    num_gridpoints=(2, 81),  # along x set by x_spacing
    upper_bound=(0.120, 0.020),
    x_spacing=[(0.0, 0.5e-3), (0.012, 0.25e-3), (0.037, 0.25e-3), (0.060, 2.0e-3)],
    x_through=(0.014, 0.016, 0.018, 0.030, 0.033, 0.036),
)
```

[Graded mesh](/beamtracer/examples/graded-mesh/) extracts a beam through an aperture and lets
it drift: the divergence of a uniform mesh with its finest cells, with half the nodes.
[Graded beam axis](/beamtracer/example-scripts/graded-beam-axis/) compares it with uniform
meshes on a planar extraction followed by a drift: the beam of a uniform 0.25 mm mesh with
40 % of its nodes. 2D only, and not with adaptive refinement.
[Mesh refinement](/beamtracer/examples/mesh-refinement/) refines a box over the meniscus and
the aperture instead.

## Electrodes of several shapes

An electrode is one entry of `objects`: one name and one potential. `ObjectParams.union`
makes one electrode of several shapes (`ShapeParams`, the simgeometry shapes of
`ObjectParams`) at one potential, built as simgeometry's Boolean union (`|`) of the shapes.
The field solve, the hits on electrodes and the per-electrode diagnostics such as
`model.surface_fields()` see it as one electrode. A tube with half-round ends:

```python
from beamtracer import ObjectParams, ShapeParams

tube = ObjectParams.union(
    [
        ShapeParams("rectangle", midpoint=(40e-3, 6e-3), side_lengths=(6e-3, 2e-3)),
        ShapeParams("circle", midpoint=(37e-3, 6e-3), radius=1e-3),
        ShapeParams("circle", midpoint=(43e-3, 6e-3), radius=1e-3),
    ],
    bc_value=15e3,
)
config = config.replace(objects={"middle": tube})
```

In the configuration-file form the shapes are listed under `shapes`, each without a `bc`:

```python
{
    "type": "union",
    "shapes": [
        {
            "type": "rectangle",
            "midpoint": ["40 mm", "6 mm"],
            "side_lengths": ["6 mm", "2 mm"],
        },
        {
            "type": "circle",
            "midpoint": ["37 mm", "6 mm"],
            "radius": "1 mm",
        },
        {
            "type": "circle",
            "midpoint": ["43 mm", "6 mm"],
            "radius": "1 mm",
        },
    ],
    "bc": {"type": "dirichlet", "potential": "15 kV"},
}
```

## Trajectories that change state

In the default `mode="fractions"` a trajectory carries the fractions of its particles in
each state and feels the force of their mean charge. That is right where the field is weak,
as in a neutralizer, but not where it separates the states: between the grids of an
accelerator an H⁻ stripped by the residual gas continues as an atom with the energy it had
gained there, in the direction it had there, while the ions go on being accelerated.

`ChargeChangingParams(..., mode="analog")` follows that: every trajectory is in one state and
carries an optical depth drawn from an exponential distribution, which the charge-changing
rate of its state, $\sum_t n_t \sum_j \sigma_t(s \to j)$, uses up along its path. Where it runs
out the trajectory changes to state $j$ with probability proportional to $n_t \sigma_t(s \to j)$
and draws a new depth. Its force is that of its actual charge. The fractions at the planes are
then 0 or 1, and everything else (`charge_tallies`, `state_moments`) works as in the fractions
mode, whose result is the mean over the trajectories; `charge_tallies.events` counts the
sampled events. With `splitting=k` a trajectory splits at each of its first `split_events`
events into `k` trajectories of a `k`-th of its current, which draw their new states
independently: more trajectories in the rarer states for the same number launched. The random
numbers come from `seed` and the MPI rank, drawn afresh on every trace, so the Vlasov
iteration still converges.

The densities of the targets are numbers or profiles along $x$,
`{"x": [...], "density": [...]}`, linear between the points: for the gas between the grids,
sampled from a neutral-transport run (picnic's `NeutralParticleTransport`) or from the
conductances of the apertures with
`beamtracer.solver.charge_changing.conductance_profile(apertures, upstream, downstream)`
(free-molecular flow through round apertures with Clausing's transmission probability, the
gas uniform in the gaps).

```python
from beamtracer.solver.charge_changing import conductance_profile

gas = conductance_profile(
    [
        (0.0, 0.001, 0.007),
        (0.006, 0.011, 0.004),
        (0.02, 0.03, 0.007),
    ],  # x0, x1, radius (m)
    upstream=1e20,  # m^-3, in the source
    downstream=1e18,  # in the tank
)
charge_changing = ChargeChangingParams(
    {"H2": {"-": {"0": 7e-21, "+": 3e-22}, "0": {"+": 2e-21}}},  # placeholders
    {"H2": gas},
    mode="analog",
    splitting=4,
)
```

## A neutralizer

`beamtracer.neutralizer.NeutralizerChannel` traces the beam through a channel whose gas and
plasma come from a global model ([carafe](https://github.com/max-models/carafe),
the `neutralizer` extra); the plasma takes its beam sources from the traced paths, and the
two are iterated to a fixed point. It gives what the global model alone cannot: the
transmission of every charge state, the beam lost on the walls, the divergence of the
neutrals. `NeutralizerChannel(..., mode="analog")` traces it with one state per trajectory.

## Examples

- [Graded mesh](/beamtracer/examples/graded-mesh/) and
  [mesh refinement](/beamtracer/examples/mesh-refinement/) extract a beam through an aperture
  on a mesh that is fine only where the field changes fast.
- [Graded beam axis](/beamtracer/example-scripts/graded-beam-axis/) compares graded, refined
  and uniform meshes on a planar extraction and a long drift.
- [Electrostatic quadrupole](/beamtracer/examples/quadrupole-3d/) traces a proton beam
  through a quadrupole on a 3D mesh: focusing in one plane, defocusing in the other.
- [Relativistic electrons](/beamtracer/example-scripts/relativistic-electrons/) accelerates
  electrons to 1 MeV and lets them gyrate in a magnetic field, traced classically and with
  `relativistic=True`.

The package also has scripts in `beamtracer.examples` (the `examples` extra), each run with
`python -m beamtracer.examples.<name>`: `beam_optics_extraction_2d`, a small planar proton
extraction, the entry point; and `negative_ion_extraction_optics`, a round H⁻ beamlet and its
electrons, with a scan of the extraction voltage.

## Units and conventions

SI units in the solver; the configuration takes charges in e, masses in u and energies and
temperatures in eV, and converts them to C, kg and J. The beam moves along $+x$; in
cylindrical geometry $y$ is the radius $r$ and a trajectory stands for a ring, in planar
geometry a current is per metre of depth. Currents are magnitudes: the sign of the space
charge is that of the particles' charge.

## Not in the model

- No time dependence. Relativity only in the tracer (`relativistic=True`); the normalised
  emittance uses $\beta$ only.
- No planar or non-axisymmetric magnetic fields in 2D.
- The source plasma is not modelled: the beams' current densities are inputs, the Boltzmann
  species has no inertia, flow or sheath, and the meniscus is where its density falls off.
- No collisions besides the [charge changes](/beamtracer/charge-changes/) (no scattering,
  energy loss or secondaries).
- No convergence safeguards: an unconverged Vlasov iteration or Newton solve returns its last
  iterate, flagged but without a warning.

## Verification

Besides the checks of the method cards (six closed forms against SymPy rules, seven
discretisations against their order of convergence), the tests compare the field with the
coaxial and uniformly charged cylinders, the Child-Langmuir diode with $-V (x/d)^{4/3}$, and
Busch's theorem through a solenoid.
